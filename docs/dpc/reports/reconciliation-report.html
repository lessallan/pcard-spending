Method and validation

  • Read-only source review: 24 monthly management reports and one general-ledger workbook. The workbook contains 15,586 dated accounting rows across 103 account/group sections; these are not 15,586 distinct purchases. Double-entry lines were not counted as spending.
  • Used integer cents, kept credits and journal entries, and excluded report subtotals from transaction sums. Twelve ledger rows have blank Amount cells; each was retained as a zero movement only after its unchanged running balance was verified. Original source remains unchanged.
  • Reconstructed month-end balance-sheet balances from the ledger opening balances plus dated movements. Reconstructed income statements using current-month and fiscal-year-to-date movements; FY25 additionally uses October amounts carried in the opening balances. October transaction-level completeness is not established.
  • Exact account numbers were the primary mapping keys. Named unnumbered accounts were matched by name. Nonzero ledger accounts absent from a report were explicitly included rather than silently dropped. Parent summary rows were excluded. Report-only lease accounts remain flagged as absent from the ledger.
  • Added the normal fiscal-year earnings rollover to retained earnings, preventing an artificial $450,056.17 discrepancy. Calculated current fiscal-year net revenue rather than treating it as a missing ledger account.
  • All 100 account-total controls and all 15,586 running balances match to the cent. All 24 reconstructed balance sheets satisfy assets = liabilities + equity, including current earnings. All extracted monthly and YTD income-statement detail sums match their printed revenue, expense and net totals. All extracted asset detail sums match printed total assets. September 2025’s key net-income page was visually checked.
  • The main financial statements were compared; budget comparisons, aging schedules, receipt completeness, and bank-statement-to-ledger reconciliation are separate work. This is a reproducible reconciliation, not an audit opinion or a determination that an expense is appropriate.
  • This is a standalone accounting reconciliation, not an import into the purchase dataset. No prior merchant/expense decisions were reused or migrated. Destination Panama City’s empty purchase baseline and ledger were left unchanged.
