Revenue

Where the money comes from

The General Fund supports day to day city services, including police, fire, street maintenance and recreation. Water and sewer, garbage collection and other fee funded services have their own funds.

Major General Fund revenue sources
SourceWhere it comes fromFY 2027 adopted
City property taxesProperty owners pay this tax based on taxable property value: the value used for taxes after exemptions. The city’s share helps pay for everyday services and supplies about 32% of this General Fund budget. See the city budget$19,750,649
Merchant feeRetail businesses pay the city a fee equal to 1% of their retail sales, subject to the city’s rules. Businesses may pass it on to customers, so you may see it on a receipt. One percent is $1 for each $100 of sales. Read the merchant fee rules$13,750,000
Fire service assessmentA separate charge on eligible properties that helps pay for fire protection. It is based on the city’s assessment rules, rather than the property’s dollar value. It supports the Fire Department. Read how the fire assessment works$6,059,224
Electric utility service taxA city tax on electricity purchased by customers in the city. The electric company collects it through customers’ bills and passes the city tax money to the city. This budget line helps fund city services. Read about utility service taxes$5,000,000
Half cent sales tax distributionFlorida shares part of its state sales-tax collections with cities and counties. This is Panama City’s share for local services. “Half-cent” is the program’s name; this entry is not an extra half-cent charge added by the city. Bay County’s infrastructure surtax is a separate program. Read the state sharing rules$4,630,730
Transfers from other fundsMoney moved into the General Fund from other city accounts. A fund is an account set aside for a particular purpose. A transfer moves existing city money; it does not collect a new payment from a resident. See the budget transfer entries$3,316,774
State revenue sharingFlorida sends eligible cities a share of state tax money under a formula set by law. Panama City uses its payment to help fund local services, subject to the program’s spending rules. This is separate from the half-cent program. Read about state revenue sharing$2,297,824
Communications services taxCustomers pay this tax on taxable services such as phone plans, cable TV and streaming. Providers collect the tax on bills and send it to Florida, which distributes the local share. Internet access itself is exempt. Read which services are taxed$1,837,077
Local option fuel taxA tax charged by the gallon and included in fuel prices. “Local option” means Florida law lets counties impose it. Panama City receives a share to pay for allowed transportation costs, such as road work. It is a cents-per-gallon tax, not a percentage of the pump price. Read about local fuel taxes$1,423,794

City Hall

The city borrowed $16.33 million in 2018 for the renovation. The original financing still had $11.05 million outstanding at September 30, 2024. The FY 2027 adopted budget includes $1,419,570 for this note, including $1,146,000 in principal and $273,570 in interest. The separate City Hall operating account budgets $552,660.

Hurricane Michael recovery

The city refinanced its $150 million short-term recovery note in 2025. The original note had replaced $24.5 million in recovery credit lines and helped pay project costs while the city awaited grant reimbursements. The staff refinancing memo lists September 1, 2027 as the maturity date.

Housing · Reviewed November 22, 2026

Housing money and what it delivers

Panama City funds home purchases, repairs, rental help and property operations. Housing & Community Services also pays for paving, public facilities and after-school services. A household assisted is not necessarily a new home built.

How many homes were built?

A verified annual completion count is still missing. The records below show budgets, spending and reported assistance. They do not establish how many new homes were finished, occupied or added to the city’s housing supply.

Reported assistance: 9 homeownership units, 71 rental units

State annual report · printed pp. 56 and 63

Reported repairs: 15 homes in 2025

The City reports that its CRA/World Changers partnership repaired 15 homes, with CRA-funded materials and volunteer labor. Work included porches, ramps, fences and painting.

This preserves existing housing. It does not count as new construction and should not be added to the SHIP figures without checking for overlap.

City’s 2025 Year in Review
Housing Authority and disaster programs are separate

The Housing Authority describes a portfolio of 262 apartment homes, 643 Housing Choice Vouchers and 70 VASH vouchers. These describe program scale, not newly built homes or verified current voucher use. Housing Authority source.

The City’s federally funded voluntary buyout program purchases flood-prone homes and demolishes them for open space and stormwater uses. It reduces flood exposure while removing housing units. Buyout program source.

What we still need to measure results
  • Completed new homes, replacement homes and net units added, supported by final inspections and occupancy records.
  • Project-level payments, contractors, completion dates and assistance type, with duplicate funding removed.
  • Waitlist size, processing times and balances split into committed, restricted and available funds.
  • The destination of the $623,000 transfer and the reason adopted infill lines fell from $650,000 in FY2026 to $10,000 in FY2027. A budget change alone does not establish cancellation.
