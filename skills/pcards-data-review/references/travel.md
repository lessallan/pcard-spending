# Travel matching

Build event editions from official event pages: event name, edition year, dates, location, source URL, retrieval date and archived evidence. Published dates establish an event, not attendance or an individual itinerary. A property lookup establishes a property location, not why someone stayed there.

Match explicit itinerary/stay dates first, named event editions next, and independently informative cross-card notes next. Purchase dates support a booking sequence; never split at January 1 merely because a flight was bought in November. Conversely, an annual conference name alone must not merge different years.

Keep early/late sessions of the same training separate. A batch of January airfare/hotel bookings can concern multiple March sessions; equal amounts and same-day booking are insufficient to choose one. Missing dates remain missing. Baggage/seat fees can attach when a person's event-named flights, itinerary or in-trip charges corroborate them; fee purchase timing alone does not establish a trip.

Preserve explicit exclusions, shared recipients and saved reviewer overrides. Booking clerks are not necessarily travelers. Classify clearly described insurance as insurance even when its trip is unresolved; a fee with an unknown amount is different from a known fee with an unknown trip. Keep mixed-event insurance unallocated until itemized.

Before accepting a candidate, test competing cities, years, nearby sessions and people. Save exact member IDs, supporting records, missing confirmation and Likely status in the ledger. Never apply one inferred match as independent proof for the next inference. Rebuild from the reviewed snapshot plus bounded changes; full candidate regeneration is research output, not a replacement for reviewed membership.
