# People and allocations

Distinguish cardholder, named traveler/recipient, approver, booking secretary and department using an asset. Administrative names such as repeated batch labels do not identify the beneficiary. A secretary's card may book multiple officials' trips.

Use explicit full names and user-confirmed aliases first. Last names and initials need context and ambiguity checks. Avoid substring matching: Street in an address is not Josh Street; a mayor's conference does not automatically mean Mayor Branch. Preserve confirmed project aliases in the existing matcher and record their source/confirmation rather than inventing a universal fuzzy-name rule.

Shared charges require an explicit roster or bounded reviewed participant set. Equal estimated shares must sum to the full charge, remain labeled estimates, and retain the recorded payer. Do not mix accounting allocations with person shares or assign a booking clerk a share just because they paid.

Department evidence order: current official roster/assignment, explicit note or confirmed asset roster, then accounting cost center as a labeled clue. Don't promote a cardholder's department into confirmed ownership of every purchase or unit. Date the roster evidence; transfers can change interpretations on later imports.
